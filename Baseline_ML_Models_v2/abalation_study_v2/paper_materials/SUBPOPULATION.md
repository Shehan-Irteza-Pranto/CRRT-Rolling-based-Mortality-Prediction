# Sub-population Robustness Analysis — best machine-only ensemble

Does the best machine-only model discriminate consistently across clinically distinct patient
subgroups? We cluster the held-out **test** circuit-days into clinical sub-populations and report
the model's AUROC within each. Generated by `subpopulation_analysis.py` (outputs in `subpop/`).

**Model under test:** the exp_3 **ensemble:mean** (rich-classical GradientBoosting + RandomForest +
attention-CNN1D, probability-averaged) — the best machine-only model. Overall test AUROC **0.649**
(1-day) / **0.643** (2-day). Clustering is **model-independent** (clinical features only), so the
subgroups are identical for both label horizons.

---

## Method
1. **Features:** the 156 clinical variables (demographics, comorbidities, severity, labs) of the
   test circuit-days; median-imputed and standardized.
2. **Clustering:** Ward hierarchical clustering (Euclidean) → dendrogram (`subpop/dendrogram_*.png`).
3. **Number of sub-populations — read from the dendrogram, not pre-fixed.** The Ward tree shows a
   clear **2-level hierarchy**: one dominant split (largest merge-height gap, 92.2 vs ≤9.9 for all
   others) defines **Level 1 = 2** super-groups, and the next structural level defines
   **Level 2 = 4** sub-populations, which we report. (The silhouette score is monotone over K =
   2…8 — 0.18→0.24 — because this ICU population is a continuum without crisp clusters, so it is
   reported but not used to choose K.) One small outlier cluster (20 young, low-comorbidity,
   0-death patients) is flagged "not evaluable" rather than dropped.
4. **Metrics:** ensemble AUROC / AUPRC / F2 / Recall within each sub-population (val-derived max-F1
   threshold).

**Figures (`subpop/`):**
- `tsne_<case>.png` — **3-D t-SNE** of the clinical features: left = colored by sub-population
  (Level 2); right = colored by outcome (survived vs died).
- `dendrogram_<case>.png` — Ward dendrogram with the Level-1 and Level-2 cut lines.
The clustering is identical for 1-day and 2-day; the t-SNE/dendrogram apply to both.

> **Reading the dendrogram.** The leftmost branch (S0, n=258) joins the tree only at a much greater
> Ward distance (≈183) than any other merge (≤91) — this is the dominant Level-1 split and shows S0
> is by far the most distinct phenotype (hence its cleanly separated blob in the t-SNE and the
> model's best discrimination there, AUROC 0.74). The small green branch (S1, n=20) is a genuine
> clinical-outlier pocket (young, comorbidity-free, very high platelets, 0 deaths), flagged
> not-evaluable — its few leaves and the large isolated orange line reflect the real data structure,
> not a plotting artifact.

## Takeaways (paste-ready)
- The Ward dendrogram defines a **2-level clinical hierarchy** (Level 1: 2 super-groups; Level 2:
  4 sub-populations); the number of sub-populations is **read off the dendrogram**, not pre-set.
- Across the evaluable Level-2 sub-populations the best machine-only ensemble discriminates
  **consistently above chance** (1-day AUROC 0.60–0.74, mean 0.650 ± 0.075; 2-day 0.58–0.70, mean
  0.652 ± 0.063) — **no subgroup collapses to random**, supporting robustness.
- Discrimination is **highest in the preserved-albumin, lower-severity group (S0, 0.74)** and
  **lowest in the large high-lactate, low-platelet group (S3, 0.60)** — a concrete, reportable
  limitation.
- The **3-D t-SNE** (`subpop/tsne_*.png`) shows S0 as a cleanly separated cluster with the other
  groups forming the continuum; the outcome panel shows deaths concentrated in the sicker S2/S3
  region.
